Trade Terms, Property Manager Billing & Tax Write-Offs

  • Homeowner Residential Terms: 'Due Upon Completion — Cash, Check, Zelle, Venmo, or Card accepted.'
  • Property Management / HOA Terms: 'Net 15 Days · Job Address: [Unit Address] · Work Order #WO-4102.'
  • Separate Materials vs. Labor: Many US states tax marked-up materials differently from repair labor — itemizing Parts and Labor on separate rows keeps your sales tax clean!
  • Trade Tax Deductions: Work truck/van mileage ($0.70/mi IRS standard rate or actual fuel/repairs), Milwaukee/DeWalt tools, Home Depot material runs, general liability insurance, and work boots.

Send an Estimate or Driveway Paid Receipt in 45 Seconds

  1. Before buying materials on larger jobs, select 'Quotation / Estimate' mode to send a written price and collect a 50% materials deposit.
  2. When the job wraps up, switch to 'Invoice' (or 'Paid Receipt' once the homeowner pays via Zelle/check/cash) and list the Service Address in Client Details.
  3. Itemize your Service Call minimum, additional labor hours, and Home Depot / Lowe's materials on clear separate lines.
  4. Tap 'Print / Save Vector PDF' on your phone or laptop and text or email the clean PDF to the homeowner or landlord (landlords love itemized PDFs for Schedule E property write-offs!).

2 Classic Handyman Billing Mistakes

  • Underpricing 20-minute 'quick fixes' without a Trip Minimum: Driving 35 minutes each way + hardware store pickup eats 2 hours; always establish a clear $110–$150 Service Call minimum.
  • Not giving landlords an itemized unit address receipt: Property managers hire the handyman who sends clean, unit-labeled PDFs ready for owner statements!

Always. Separating Parts/Materials from Labor builds immediate homeowner trust and helps both you and your CPA apply local state sales-tax rules accurately.
